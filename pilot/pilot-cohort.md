# Pilot Cohort (Dec 2025 – May 2026)

Before launching Lead Shop, David Ebner ran a test cohort with a small leadership team. This page keeps what's useful for designing Lead Shop. The participating organization, its people, and its business details are intentionally left out.

## Format

- **Group:** A small leadership team from one organization
- **Cadence:** Every two weeks, remote, about one hour
- **Agenda pattern:** Roundup → What's new → Assignment review → Book discussion → New assignment → Next up
- **Extras in the plan:** Monthly 1:1s, office hours, a shared chat channel, and twice-yearly field trips. The field trips never happened.

## Books covered, in order

| Book | Period | Coverage |
|------|--------|----------|
| *Start with Why* | Dec 2025 – Jan 2026 | Whole book (Parts 1–6) |
| *Tribal Leadership* | Jan – Feb 2026 | Parts 1–4 |
| *Unreasonable Hospitality* | Feb – Mar 2026 | Whole book |
| *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People* | Apr – May 2026 | Intro and Habits 1–3; Habits 4–7 not reached |

The original plan also included *Delivering Happiness*, *The Toyota Way*, and *Creative Confidence* (see [`../program/book-list.md`](../program/book-list.md)).

## Assignments that were used

| Book | Assignment |
|------|------------|
| *Start with Why* | Build your Golden Circle. Label each team member as a Why, How, or What person. |
| *Tribal Leadership* Part 2 | Tribal Stage Audit & Upgrade Plan (README §4.6) |
| *Tribal Leadership* Parts 3–4 | Form 3–4 triad teams. |
| *Unreasonable Hospitality* | Brainstorm 4 unreasonable-hospitality actions to do before an upcoming key event. Set up a hospitality committee with a deadline. Have teammates read a chosen chapter (Ch. 18). |
| *7 Habits* intro | For two weeks, catch yourself using reactive vs. proactive language and write it down. Fill out the Circles worksheet ([`tools/circles-of-control/`](../tools/circles-of-control/index.html)) in the order Concern → Influence → Control. |
| *7 Habits* Habits 1–3 | Funeral Exercise: which of the four eulogies (family, friends, coworkers, community) would be easiest to write about you today, and which hardest? What does the gap say about where your time and attention go? |

### Example: proactive vs. reactive rewrite (from the pilot)
- *Reactive:* "I have to have a meeting with [person]."
- *Proactive:* "We can send regular updates on the vision to each audience: leadership, the core team, and outside partners."

## Facilitator insights worth reusing

These came up in pilot discussions. Each works as a discussion prompt or a callback in Lead Shop.

- **Values mean nothing until they're challenged,** until standing up for them costs you something. *(Tribal Leadership, core values)*
- **A true leader trains someone else to become a leader.**
- **"Don't come to me with problems. Come with three ideas to solve them."** *(Turn the Ship Around!, stewardship delegation)*
- **"You're paid for your opinion."** Everyone should have one. It's the leader's job to decide and the team's job to bring the information.
- **Every responsibility has an owner and a second person** who can step in.
- **People should know which decisions are theirs to make** and which need approval. Many people don't believe they're allowed to decide.
- **Fix the process before adding a person.** With a good process, you may not need the hire. It's better to hire someone to make the process better.
- **Handling repeated questions without frustration:** "I'm losing time repeating this, and I'm sure asking costs you time too. How can I help you remember so neither of us loses time?"
- **Get 100% on the things that don't take skill** (task management, operations) so your energy goes to the things that do (relationships, writing).
- **"One cool thing" on Mondays:** a whole-team ritual to start the week and build belonging. *(Tribal Leadership, Unreasonable Hospitality)*
- **Feel, felt, found:** show you understand how someone feels by relating it to your own experience. "When I felt X, here's what I found." Works in sales too: play back the challenges and goals you heard. *(Crucial Conversations; 7 Habits, Habit 5)*
- **Always have an agenda.**
- **The Four Quadrants as a diagnostic:** for each friction area, name the owner, the cost, the business impact, and which quadrant it *should* live in vs. where it actually ends up (usually Q1).

## Lessons for Lead Shop

- **Pace.** Meeting every two weeks covered about 3½ books in 5 months. Lead Shop meets weekly, so the 22-week spring cohort has 22 sessions. The pilot had about a dozen.
- **Concrete assignments with deadlines work best:** triad teams, a committee with a real deadline, a Golden Circle for each team member.
- **The interactive tool got used.** Keep building tools like it (see README §13).
- **Participants wanted more on management mechanics**, which led to the suggestions of *Scaling People*, *Scaling Up*, *Crucial Conversations*, *Getting Things Done*, and *Turn the Ship Around!*.
- **Planned extras slipped.** Field trips and 1:1s didn't happen. Decide which extras Lead Shop actually commits to.
